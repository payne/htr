⚖ FCC Part 97 — Key Operating Rules
STATION IDENTIFICATION
▶ID with callsign every 10 minutes and at the end of each communication session
▶ID must use English, CW, or phone — callsign only (not tactical/net names alone)
▶Tactical names (e.g., "Net Control") are ok — but FCC callsign ID still required
POWER LIMITS
▶Technician HF bands: 200 W PEP max (80/40/15/10m)
▶All VHF/UHF bands: 1,500 W PEP (all license classes)
▶Always use minimum power necessary for reliable communication (§97.313)
PROHIBITED TRANSMISSIONS
✕Music transmissions (except incidental in retransmitted audio)
✕Obscene, indecent, or profane language
✕False / deceptive signals (false SOS/MAYDAY except genuine emergency)
✕Broadcasting (one-way entertainment transmissions to the public)
✕Communications in return for payment (business for hire)
✕Encrypted messages intended to obscure their meaning
OTHER KEY RULES
▶Third-party traffic: only with countries having a US third-party agreement
▶Unattended transmitters: control operator remains responsible at all times
▶Emergency comms: may use any frequency with minimum power — "MAYDAY"
▶Licenses expire every 10 years; may renew up to 2 years early
▶Unlicensed persons (third parties) may speak under licensed op supervision

⚠ RF & Electrical Safety
RF EXPOSURE (FCC §97.13)
▶Controlled env (operators): higher MPE limits permitted
▶Uncontrolled env (public): stricter, lower MPE limits apply
▶Evaluation required above certain power thresholds per band
▶Distance is the best protection — never sit near a transmitting antenna
▶Duty cycle matters: CW < SSB < FM for avg RF exposure
✕NEVER transmit into open-ended feedline or mismatched antenna
ELECTRICAL SAFETY
▶Ground all station equipment — single-point bonding at station
▶Use GFI/GFCI outlets near station, especially near batteries/AC
✕NEVER work on powered equipment — discharge caps before touching
✕Disconnect and ground antennas/feed lines during thunderstorms
✕NEVER climb towers before/during a storm or in wet conditions
▶Tube amps: plate voltages often 2–4 kV — retain charge after power off
▶Always discharge high-voltage caps with resistor-on-stick before work
🌐 Propagation Modes
Ground Wave — MF/low HF; hugs Earth's surface; AM broadcast coverage
Sky Wave (Ionospheric) — HF; reflects off ionosphere for long-haul DX
D layer: Absorbs HF daytime  ·  E layer: ~90 km, daytime  ·  F1/F2: 200–400 km, main DX
Sporadic-E (Es) — patchy E layer; enables surprise 6m & 2m DX
Line-of-Sight (LOS) — VHF/UHF; direct path; typical local FM/repeater
Tropospheric Ducting — temperature inversions; 100s of miles on VHF/UHF
Auroral — VHF scatter off aurora; raspy/distorted tone; near polar regions
Meteor Scatter — short bursts via ionized meteor trails; VHF
EME (Moonbounce) — reflect off Moon; extreme DX; VHF/UHF
